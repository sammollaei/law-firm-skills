# Benchmark a practice

Narrow the craft to a specific artifact and result, such as an appointment reminder and attendance rate. Read the user's permitted current version. Record its baseline or state that it is unknown.

Identify comparators using documented outcomes with dates, populations and conditions. Inspect primary material such as a published study, an example form or a recording the user may review. Capture a precise source location and explain what the example demonstrates. A popular account, promotional claim or unavailable source supplies no verified outcome.

Look for techniques repeated independently or supported by a controlled comparison. Separate a shared practice from evidence that it caused the result. Report differences in audience, staffing, consent and resources that affect transfer. A promising anecdote can justify a small test; it cannot establish who is best.

Compare the current artifact with each supported technique. Identify what it already does well. Recommend at most three changes with the evidence, a small reversible trial, success metric and observation that would reverse the recommendation. Where the evidence is weak, keeping the existing practice and naming the missing comparison is a valid result.

Use original wording. Keep private records out of examples. Changes to live intake, client-facing communications, spending or staff processes require their own review and authorization.
