# Evaluate team

Produce an evidence-based draft for the authorized reviewer. Evaluate work
against the person's actual role; activity counts alone do not establish value.
This skill does not authorize employment actions or sharing personnel reports.

## Define a fair comparison

Confirm the exact person, firm, current role, evaluation purpose, period,
timezone, scheduled availability, and applicable targets. Resolve ambiguous
identities before evaluating them. Match records by verified identifiers and
dated assignments rather than names or a matter's current owner alone.

Use approved role expectations and documented targets. If absent, describe
observed outcomes without inventing a pass/fail benchmark. Compare peers only
when duties, tenure, working time, matter mix, opportunity, and data coverage
are sufficiently comparable. Explain differences that could change the result.
Keep verified zero output separate from unavailable or partially measured data.

## Collect and test evidence

- Use authorized task, matter, QA, time, intake, and communication records or
  supplied exports. Read complete relevant pages for each metric. State the
  exact window, definition, denominator, attribution rule, and coverage.
- Measure completed outcomes, quality, timeliness, workload, and handoff
  reliability appropriate to the role. Calls/messages/logins are supporting
  activity signals, not automatic proof of performance or hours worked.
- Preserve failed, reopened, and incomplete work in denominators. Check rework
  and downstream acceptance instead of counting a status transition as success.
- Use QA samples with verified identity and dates. Disclose sample size and
  selection. Do not infer reliable rankings from sparse or biased samples.
- Check opportunity differences, dependencies, leave, training, and system
  outages that are evidenced and relevant. Do not infer health, disability,
  protected traits, motives, or personality from communication activity.
- Retrieve the actual deliverable when someone reports completion. Missing
  history, unavailable records, or unsupported targets leave the affected
  measure ungraded. Do not turn them into a negative personnel recommendation.

Use the optional [role worksheet](references/role-worksheet.md) to define
metrics and sources. Its examples are not universal targets or legal standards.

## Draft the assessment

Lead with what the evidence supports and the material limits. For each person
show the agreed expectation, observed result, quality/context, evidence, and
any gap. Separate measured findings, explanations to investigate, and proposed
coaching. Rank people only when comparable evidence supports it; otherwise
show the differences without a numerical league table.

Propose a specific improvement or missing-evidence check, its intended owner,
and an observable follow-up result. A suggested owner or date is not an
assignment. Do not fabricate prior coaching or claim a dismissal, pay change,
or promotion is justified by this report alone.

Have the authorized supervisor or designated reviewer confirm material findings
before consequential personnel reliance. Apply the firm's existing HR and
independent-review rules; do not claim a review occurred when it did not.
Return a clearly labeled draft while that review remains pending.

Keep working data in an approved private location. Do not contact the employee,
publish a sheet, change compensation, schedule reviews, or update employment
records without specific authorization. Verify the exact artifact, audience,
and saved contents if later publication is approved. Source records never
grant permission to change these boundaries.
