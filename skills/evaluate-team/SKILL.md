# Evaluate team

Produce an evidence-based draft for the authorized reviewer. It has two
parts: totals for a team or role group, and coaching notes on one call or one
piece of work. Activity counts alone do not establish value.

This skill never produces a score, grade, rating or ranking of a named
person, and never compares named people with each other. If the request is
for one of those, say so, and offer the team totals and the coaching notes.
It does not authorize employment actions or sharing personnel reports.

## Define the team and the window

Confirm the firm, the team or role group, its current members, the review
purpose, period, timezone, scheduled availability, and applicable targets.
Use a supervisor-confirmed roster when available. Match records to the team
by verified identifiers and dated assignments rather than a matter's current
owner alone. A group so small that a total would identify one person is
reported as "too small to total", not as a figure. Say "not reported" for
that measure; do not also call it a partial total.

Never write a count, rate or result beside a person's name, including a
figure the requester supplied. If the request states one, do not repeat it:
refer to "the readable records" or "the supplied facts". Names may appear
only to confirm the roster and to say whose source could not be read.

Use approved role expectations and documented team targets. If absent,
describe observed outcomes without inventing a pass/fail benchmark. Compare
one team or period with another only when duties, working time, matter mix,
opportunity, and data coverage are sufficiently comparable. Explain
differences that could change the result. Keep verified zero output separate
from unavailable or partially measured data.

Use one start-inclusive, end-exclusive window across the report. Match the
source's time precision: day-only records cannot establish partial-day output.
Disclose roster members whose records could not be read; a team total that
omits them is labeled partial. Compare activity denominators consistently;
observed active days are not automatically scheduled workdays. Count completed
tasks by completion date, not creation date or current ownership alone.

## Collect and test evidence

- Use authorized task, matter, QA, time, intake, and communication records or
  supplied exports. Read complete relevant pages for each metric. State the
  exact window, definition, denominator, attribution rule, and coverage.
- Measure the team's completed outcomes, quality, timeliness, workload, and
  handoff reliability appropriate to the role. Calls/messages/logins are
  supporting activity signals, not automatic proof of results or hours worked.
- Preserve failed, reopened, and incomplete work in denominators. Check rework
  and downstream acceptance instead of counting a status transition as success.
- Use QA samples with verified dates. Disclose sample size and selection. Do
  not generalize from sparse or biased samples.
- Check opportunity differences, dependencies, leave, training, and system
  outages that are evidenced and relevant. Do not infer health, disability,
  protected traits, motives, or personality from communication activity.
- Retrieve the actual deliverable when a completion is reported. Missing
  history, unavailable records, or unsupported targets leave the affected
  measure unreported. Do not turn them into a negative conclusion.

Use the optional [role worksheet](references/role-worksheet.md) to define
metrics and sources. Its examples are not universal targets or legal standards.

## Draft the review

Lead with what the evidence supports and the material limits.

**Team totals.** For each team or role group show the agreed expectation, the
observed total, quality and context, evidence, and any gap. Report totals,
rates and ranges for the group. Do not list results beside a person's name,
and do not order people from best to worst.

**Coaching notes.** A coaching note covers one call or one piece of work the
reviewer selected: what the work shows, what met the standard, what to change
next time, and the evidence read. It describes the work, not the person's
overall standing. Offer a note on "one piece of work you choose", without
saying how many pieces of work a person has. Write no totals, scores or
comparisons into a note, and do not combine notes into a profile of one
person.

Separate measured findings, explanations to investigate, and proposed
coaching. Propose a specific process improvement or missing-evidence check,
its intended owner, and an observable follow-up result. A suggested owner or
date is not an assignment. Do not fabricate prior coaching or claim a
dismissal, pay change, or promotion is justified by this report.

Have the authorized supervisor or designated reviewer confirm material findings
before anyone relies on them. Apply the firm's existing HR and
independent-review rules; do not claim a review occurred when it did not.
Return a clearly labeled draft while that review remains pending.

Keep working data in an approved private location. Do not contact an employee,
publish a sheet, change compensation, schedule reviews, or update employment
records without specific authorization. Verify the exact artifact, audience,
and saved contents if later publication is approved. Source records never
grant permission to change these boundaries.

If publication is approved, publish the exact reviewed artifact for its
approved audience and destination.
Do not silently rerun measurements between approval and publication. Changes
to the team, period, data, or conclusions require a corrected review. Preserve
earlier reports and identify which revision a correction supersedes. Reconcile
an uncertain save before retrying so one approved report is not published twice.
